About

How I got here

I started my career as a software engineer before moving into product management — giving me a firsthand understanding of how to work closely with the engineers.

At Animoca Brands, I lead product across the money and stablecoin layer — building a financial infrastructure platform for enterprises and AI agents to hold, grow and move money easily. Before that, I was Head of Product at KyberSwap, one of DeFi's leading trading protocols, where I scaled the product to over $100B in trading volume. Earlier in my career I also held product and engineering roles at Thought Machine, Ubisoft, and Credit Suisse.

I've worked across early-stage startups, high-growth scaleups moving fast, and large institutions with complex stakeholder structures. I've built product teams from scratch, put in place the processes that let teams ship with clarity, and know how to align stakeholders across engineering, business, and executive levels.

Two things occupy me right now. First: AI agents are becoming autonomous actors, and the financial infrastructure for that doesn't yet fully exist — payment rails, programmable wallets, policy engines. This is something I'm actively exploring. Second: I've embedded AI deeply into my own product workflows, making me a faster product manager and giving me the space to prototype and validate concepts independently.

Philosophy

How I build

The mental models and convictions I bring to every product I work on.

01

Know the user before knowing the product

The biggest mistakes in product come from building for an assumed user rather than a real one. I spend time understanding who we are building for, their context, their frustrations, and how they actually make decisions, before committing to a direction.

02

Complexity is a UX debt

Every layer of complexity we expose is a user we have already lost. I love the challenge of simplifying hard products for consumers, taking something technically dense and making it feel obvious. My job is to absorb the hard parts so the product feels simple.

03

Validate before we build

The gap between what users say and what they do is where the real insight lives. I try and push for lightweight validation and prototypes before committing to a build. It's the cheapest way to find out we are wrong.

04

Spec before the first mockup

I write the document before the first mockup is drawn. Specs force clarity, surface assumptions nobody had bothered to say out loud, and help teams move faster. They also force the hard decisions early, before they get expensive.

05

Saying no is a product strategy

Every yes is a commitment of time, attention, and engineering. The roadmap is defined as much by what we cut as what we ship. This is something I'm still working on, but getting better at.

06

Own one number

Every product I run has a north star metric. Everything else is context. Clarity on what success looks like is the difference between a team that ships with purpose and one that just ships.
